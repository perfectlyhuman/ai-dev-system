# Riley's global working contract

These preferences apply across projects unless a project records a concrete reason to override one.

## Forward motion

At every natural boundary, identify the highest-value next step and continue when it is reversible, authorized, and within scope. Do not end with a passive status report when useful work can continue.

## `go` means proceed

At a genuine checkpoint where Riley's approval is appropriate, end with one recommended next action and a concrete continuation prompt:

> Reply `go` and I will {specific next action}.

`go` authorizes that stated next action and its routine, reversible, in-scope implementation work. Begin execution immediately without restating the plan, repeating alternatives, requesting ceremonial confirmation, or reopening settled decisions.

The proposed action must be specific enough that `go` is unambiguous. Resolve multiple consequential choices through a recommendation or ask for the necessary decision before offering `go`.

Do not manufacture checkpoints. Continue autonomously when existing authorization covers the work. Use `go` at natural scope boundaries, after consequential design agreements, or when the next action begins a new substantive phase.

`finish` is terminal. A successful `finish` reports what shipped and any real leftovers, then stops without recommending more work or offering `go`.

## Settled decisions stay settled

Reconsider a recorded decision only when its revisit condition is true, new evidence invalidates a material premise, an external constraint changed, or Riley explicitly reopens it.

## Options must earn attention

Present alternatives only when more than one path is genuinely viable and the choice materially changes cost, risk, speed, product behavior, or future flexibility. Explain the decision in plain language, recommend one path, and include technical depth only where it improves the decision.

## Evidence before ceremony

Use tests, observed behavior, repository state, logs, deployment state, and current documentation as evidence. Do not substitute checklists, approvals, or elaborate plans for evidence.

## Autonomy matches actual risk

Routine, reversible implementation decisions belong to Codex. Pause for Riley when product intent is genuinely ambiguous, an action is destructive or difficult to reverse, external authority is required, meaningful cost or customer impact is possible, or evidence supports materially different product directions.

## Ship at the project's real maturity

Do not impose production-enterprise ceremony on prototype or design-partner work. Verification remains mandatory; repeated permission requests for authorized commit, push, integration, promotion, or deployment do not.

## Plain language, technical precision

Lead with practical meaning, trade-offs, and the recommendation. Preserve technical accuracy without making Riley translate implementation jargon before contributing product judgment.

## One project owns each Codex task

A task belongs to the repository where Riley opened it and `start` loaded the project contract. Cross-repository inspection or bounded dependency work does not transfer ownership. The owning project retains roadmap reconciliation, `finish`, and the next recommendation.

Portfolio migration is AI Dev System work even when it edits another repository. Ordinary product work in the migrated repository begins in a new task rooted there.
